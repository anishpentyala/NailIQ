Dear Dr. Nelson and colleagues,

I am developing NailIQ, a student research project that screens photographs for
visible nail signs. It reports whether an appearance is outside a typical range
or whether a photograph cannot be analyzed; it does not diagnose systemic disease.

Your 2018 study photographed 503 outpatients and collected independent physician
assessments of Terry's nails. Could a permitted, de-identified subset of the existing
photographs and assessments be made available for noncommercial research?

The useful records would be original finger photographs, anonymous patient identifiers,
and each reviewer's present/absent/indeterminate sign assessment, retaining disagreement
and ungradable images. Exact counts and collection details would help us assess whether
the data are appropriate. We do not need patient identities or medical records.

We would also need the applicable consent/use conditions and permission, if available,
to train and evaluate research models. We can keep raw photographs private, retain
attribution and separate patients between training and evaluation. We would not assume
permission for public/commercial app use or redistribute photographs.

This request concerns data and reviews already collected; it does not ask your team
to label new photographs or provide a clinical service.
